Deep-research run, ultradeep mode, 2026-10-07 · worktree `research/oop-design-scouts` (base `main` eb0462b, 6.4.2-alepar4.16) · lead: Herdr agent `oop-research`.

## Executive Summary

**Direct answer.** The OOP practices the human partner named are well-founded *maintainability hypotheses*, not laws: the primary literature supports hiding likely-to-change decisions behind narrow interfaces, behavioral (contract-level) substitutability for hierarchies, force-driven rather than habitual patterns, and language-canonical error handling; but the empirical record is correlational, size-confounded, mixed in controlled experiments, and language-dependent, and no line-count or depth threshold is validated. Two consequences follow for Superpowers. First, design-time incentives work only as concrete artefacts — declared interfaces, owners, error contracts, named variation axes — because generic design prompting has small, model-dependent effects with trade-offs. Second, review-time enforcement has to be precision-first and, where it targets design, anchored to stated contracts or observable consequences: in recorded roasts, interface findings were confirmed when a written contract existed while other design findings mostly ended rejected, demoted, or unverified (A4-F18), and in a large 2026 preprint most agentic review comments were rejected, chiefly as false positives or conflicts with intentional design trade-offs (F8).

**What to do (detail in [recommendations.md](recommendations.md), v2 after an independent critique).** Do not add a new super-auto stage or design pass. Measure first: deterministic lane attribution, bead timing, and an evaluation set with held-out defects. Then ship only cheap, compile-checked or eval-precedented changes, each on its own branch with its own eval: harden seam-contract content (error variants, stub inventory, stability marker, no signature change in the contract's own fix pass), port the `Interfaces:` block to the super-code planner with provisional entries, and add Minor design tags to the task reviewer. Treat contract-anchored review as the first hypothesis to test — starting with the zero-code probe of giving PR roasts the spec as stated intent — and run any new lane in shadow mode before it can drive fixes. Pilot the unowned error-lifecycle classes inside the existing `correctness` lane with a language canon table, leaving single-statement patterns to linters. Defer proactive seam-contract triggers until stacked-consumer drift is covered.

**Confidence and stop.** Every high-priority facet reached direct evidence with counterevidence; seven of nine high-priority facets are contested on material points. Retrieval stopped as **budget-exhausted**: residual probes kept finding new relevant 2026 material, so saturation was not claimed (see Limitations). All recommendations are unevaluated hypotheses.

## Main Analysis

### F1 — Coherent abstraction models as functionality grows
<!-- facet: F1 -->

The founding criterion is about change, not about classes. System details that are likely to change independently should be the secrets of separate modules, and interfaces should carry only assumptions considered unlikely to change [2]. <!-- claim: b8ec3869468d8877; evidence: faf49268c78ef0e5; source: e9c889ed967319c9 --> The empirical support is correlational but consistent. In both studied systems, the tightly-coupled core components cost significantly more to maintain than loosely-coupled peripheral components [7]. <!-- claim: 2faba3834e04247f; evidence: f41a9395e590e661; source: 111a399701810c52 --> Logical dependencies explained most of the variance in fault proneness, while all dependencies increased it [12]. <!-- claim: b23a72c44f0fc7ac; evidence: 9d004f4b95947b7f; source: 6ac14d85d17ac41e --> At the same time, the measurement tools are weak: There are no thresholds for contemporary object-oriented metrics, including class size [5]. <!-- claim: 0a4f25a6c7d67aec; evidence: c4375917ce8af1f8; source: 3a4153f5400047a8 --> A small controlled experiment points the same way as the theory: Nine of twenty participants in the high modularity condition completed the modification, compared to only three of twenty in the low modularity condition [21]. <!-- claim: ae12340b121260dd; evidence: 55b4f18b0b4612ac; source: b0b34be03ea0ba16 -->

*Synthesis.* "Coherent abstraction model" is best operationalised as *each likely change lands in one place, and every interface states only what is meant to be stable*. That is checkable at design time (name the hidden decision and the promised behaviors) and at review time (phrase a finding as the change it makes expensive), and not checkable by metrics. Detail: [D1](dossiers/D1-abstraction-information-hiding.md).

### F3 — Encapsulation and hiding implementation details
<!-- facet: F3 -->

Hiding has documented costs as well as benefits. The implementation cannot always be hidden: its performance characteristics can show through, and the client programmer is limited by them [16]. <!-- claim: c27d4e6b238fce12; evidence: 4ba2ffda80e11549; source: 8427129b64174cd5 --> With a sufficient number of users of an API, all observable behaviors of a system will be depended on by somebody, whatever the contract promises [18]. <!-- claim: 9b0973c3303275ab; evidence: 98c53a1e3d51d2be; source: d4d5cfab36bc917d --> The benefit side is visible at ecosystem scale: Plug-ins depending solely on APIs had a very high source compatibility success rate compared to those depending on non-APIs [20]. <!-- claim: 67c18dd13c45bfb2; evidence: 41b4872268db3e4d; source: ef6469a7e954e34d -->

*Synthesis.* Enforcement should target exposure that consumers *now depend on* (an implementation type or mutable internal crossing a boundary) and state which observable behaviors are promised, rather than demand maximal hiding. The Law of Demeter's benefits are unproven by its own authors' account (D1). Detail: [D1](dossiers/D1-abstraction-information-hiding.md).

### F2 — Meaningful type hierarchies and polymorphism
<!-- facet: F2 -->

A meaningful hierarchy is defined by behavioral contracts, which a type checker does not prove. The pre-condition rule ensures the subtype's method can be called in any state required by the supertype [29]. <!-- claim: c5673f2181679ce2; evidence: c22df34ad94f7f3a; source: 171b777c947e90e7 --> The depth evidence conflicts: Subjects maintaining software with three levels of inheritance depth performed maintenance tasks significantly quicker than those maintaining equivalent software with no inheritance [32]. <!-- claim: 8a9dd65b18ae11e2; evidence: 0e5ffef1f6090dc4; source: 18152de2db2dd32e --> Systems without inheritance were easier to modify than corresponding systems containing three or five levels of inheritance [33]. <!-- claim: f6b8ded6802f41d1; evidence: 852708a9fb93e069; source: 7ba63c1dfa20668e --> One experiment resolves part of the conflict: The number of methods that need to be understood was the most useful factor for predicting effort, far more reliable than inheritance depth [34]. <!-- claim: 9fa710654197562a; evidence: e05f907dab6b4586; source: bf33f296577e26b9 --> Non-class-oriented languages encode the same forces differently, so review rules must adapt — for example in Go: Go interfaces generally belong in the package that uses values of the interface type, and interfaces should not be defined before they are used [44]. <!-- claim: c9179ecd18989dce; evidence: 38a24b29a72ff162; source: 7d7209181e775e43 --> and in languages with sealed hierarchies: A match-all clause risks sweeping exhaustiveness errors under the rug; an exhaustive switch without one is better when possible [52]. <!-- claim: 0fb73b0062173ee6; evidence: 307bf05745133a8a; source: 0c58c6236b070781 --> The smell evidence is itself contested: Refused Parent Bequest increases change-proneness and is the only smell in that cohort study also increasing fault-proneness [38]. <!-- claim: 0938023a42bf982e; evidence: 51d3e54b6f3f970b; source: a907cdd6b476ebc0 --> None of the 12 investigated smells was significantly associated with increased effort after adjusting for file size and the number of changes, and Refused Bequest was associated with decreased effort [67]. <!-- claim: 780333c8f68e9c46; evidence: 65543dea9c2bf467; source: 167f187364b4db61 -->

*Synthesis.* Check overrides against the supertype's stated contract and flag concrete-type switching where a closed type with an exhaustive match or a polymorphic method would replace it; treat depth only as a triage cue; never flag switches over sealed/enum types, idiomatic Go type switches, or consumer-defined small interfaces. Detail: [D2](dossiers/D2-hierarchies-polymorphism.md).

### F4 — GoF and common design patterns
<!-- facet: F4 -->

The pattern authors themselves make patterns conditional on forces. Design patterns should not be applied indiscriminately: they add levels of indirection, and a pattern should only be applied when the flexibility it affords is actually needed [261]. <!-- claim: 0a3ac11555c9bfe0; evidence: 2a6b80da183c0188; source: bf1ef2949b3ca4da --> Do not start immediately throwing patterns into a design; use them as you go and understand more of the problem, refactoring to patterns [262]. <!-- claim: 05e0056aef44e4a5; evidence: 7dfe992868b3a0a5; source: ec3a40d9899b9776 --> Controlled experiments disagree about the cost of unneeded patterns: Pre-Pat subjects required 151% more time than Pre-Alt subjects in the unneeded Observer task [249]. <!-- claim: 962216efc17d6e92; evidence: 2650d4cdac5c946e; source: 971acbc742b26220 --> The professional replication found Visitor harmful but observed no significant harm done by using the Observer [251]. <!-- claim: 4facab9b984b78e2; evidence: 6fd6a05bb6c90a5c; source: 43f31e3f79053477 --> The main effect of patterns differs across experiments and sites, and an analysis of moderators including developer experience and pattern knowledge resolves the differences [252]. <!-- claim: 2c15dcb9e0d752d5; evidence: 5352b816d913ee76; source: d08b092a935b7730 --> Patterns are also language-dependent: 16 of 23 patterns have qualitatively simpler implementation in Lisp or Dylan than in C++ for at least some uses of each pattern [266]. <!-- claim: 73a16620cb6a00ab; evidence: 08a47b05a4b5cb7b; source: b8289846e424d683 -->

*Synthesis.* Ask what variation, or what modifiability or testability gain, an indirection buys; never score pattern presence; prefer language-native constructs; land unneeded-indirection findings low. Detail: [D3](dossiers/D3-design-patterns.md).

### F5 — Methods and data at the right abstraction level; cohesion; code as documentation
<!-- facet: F5 -->

Size thresholds do not survive scrutiny as review rules. The first critical method-size value of 24 covers 70% of the distribution in the Alves et al. approach, with 36 and 63 covering 80% and 90% [61]. <!-- claim: a0f57305ee5df2e4; evidence: db05d5cebf1e5026; source: 2b0fdf810057a2dd --> For the bug-proneness indicator, most of the differences between method-size groups are within negligible and small effect sizes [61]. <!-- claim: bea4faf4933efbd7; evidence: b8c4b43a862391bc; source: 2b0fdf810057a2dd --> Controlled decomposition experiments are mixed: The influence of function decomposition on code understanding is inconclusive, suggesting that functional decomposition does not universally enhance code comprehensibility [59]. <!-- claim: 446144a3641d8c30; evidence: 08d43e28efdcab86; source: 6bae4eadb033f87b --> Contrary to expectations, most developers required more time when given the refactored code [60]. <!-- claim: 9df7a3a71dc86855; evidence: f6841b2e7baeccea; source: 2e4128d509729805 --> Most OO metrics are confounded by size: After controlling for size, none of the studied object-oriented metrics were associated with fault-proneness anymore [62]. <!-- claim: d2078dcf61e10e57; evidence: 9d9f3bb7722e7716; source: 370ae4ea452c7b80 --> The maintainability metrics were not mutually consistent, and only system size and low cohesion were strongly associated with increased maintenance effort [68]. <!-- claim: 86a44075382264d3; evidence: c6107dcc5a3b98ad; source: 473046723ee8fd76 --> Duplication is harmful only selectively: Nearly every second unintentionally inconsistent change to a clone leads to a fault [77]. <!-- claim: 7e3862b16956ecd1; evidence: a1c658ee08debb0d; source: 50aea8c0887e3136 --> Most clones are rarely changed and the number of unintentional inconsistent changes to clones is small [78]. <!-- claim: 97bc0283ba0a8f02; evidence: b3308cc040c3975a; source: 62684d4f289e3f77 --> Under-abstraction is a risk too — in one 2026 preprint: PureAI projects show lower code smell density and appear simpler, which is consistent with oversimplification associated with missing abstractions [237]. <!-- claim: 3677db0251a2e0de; evidence: 4eed6e4c3b4fb2cf; source: 19cbec92a90070bf -->

*Synthesis.* "No uber methods" is a reasonable goal but an unreliable rule; findings should cite a concrete comprehension or change cost, and the counterweight (deduplication that adds mode flags) belongs beside any duplication finding. Enforcement should not lean anti-abstraction. Detail: [D4](dossiers/D4-abstraction-level-size-measurement.md).

### F6 — Canonical error, exception, logging and cleanup practice
<!-- facet: F6 -->

The empirical case for caring is strong at the outage end. Almost all (92%) of the catastrophic system failures are the result of incorrect handling of non-fatal errors explicitly signaled in software [126]. <!-- claim: 8c7033a790406b63; evidence: 1026b1460966cd1c; source: 344401f57c206ae3 --> 25% of the catastrophic failures were caused by ignoring explicit errors, where an error handler that only logs the error also counts as ignoring it [126]. <!-- claim: 3abe5ebd053bdced; evidence: 4b5226b3be27cce6; source: 344401f57c206ae3 --> Much of it is mechanically detectable: A static checker, Aspirator, built from three simple rules, would have prevented over 30% of the catastrophic failures [126]. <!-- claim: 42961f5658fd6d9a; evidence: 1efacd9066006795; source: 344401f57c206ae3 --> It is weaker for most individual anti-patterns: The majority of exception handling anti-patterns are not significant in the models, but some anti-patterns provide significant explanatory power for post-release defects [155]. <!-- claim: f6be81dc7c6b4b17; evidence: 291701f770a74f61; source: 153c8410f827d032 --> The canon is language-specific and partly version-specific. Rust: In error types that wrap an underlying error, the underlying error should be returned by Error::source() or rendered by the Display implementation, but not both [86]. <!-- claim: 96a0ea1da5073e13; evidence: e878a39f704d0839; source: 406996ddd3038b82 --> Go: Wrap an error to expose it to callers, but do not wrap an error when doing so would expose implementation details [104]. <!-- claim: 9fb39245679c6c02; evidence: eb8392f2bd8b284e; source: 8de9f533db3604ee --> Python: Use exception chaining appropriately: raise X from Y indicates explicit replacement without losing the original traceback [117]. <!-- claim: 4d713332cd55f25d; evidence: c3a87a165cfa42c1; source: 6b1e2118b392d9ff --> Logging ownership is close to universal: Do not add any handlers other than NullHandler to your library's loggers, because configuring handlers is the prerogative of the application developer [122]. <!-- claim: 3b72145899d6ac82; evidence: a91ab1ae702823cf; source: 9af3616aa3d10c5e --> But "handle once" is contested between official sources: If you return an error, it is usually better not to log it yourself but rather let the caller handle it [110]. <!-- claim: 90e4b97798ee33ec; evidence: b11acf51038cf61d; source: 86d8597102301157 --> The most common pattern for handling Exception is to print or log the exception and then re-raise it [116]. <!-- claim: 04af6b8a3c3dc8cb; evidence: 472c9b49d045e9da; source: 8687f74540356d37 --> Several canons converge on catching only where recovery is possible: Catching an exception in a function that cannot take a meaningful recovery action leads to complexity and waste; let the exception propagate [144]. <!-- claim: f1d3845c17841b8a; evidence: 682cf211cd1b136a; source: 8117f6cdcbd0c4fa --> A crashed app is more reliable and diagnosable than an app with undefined behavior [132]. <!-- claim: 5a4c7731c1df0f8a; evidence: a3a457eedd026a07; source: c0750c248927eeb8 --> Observability conventions changed recently: Exceptions SHOULD be recorded as attributes on the LogRecord passed to the Logger emit operations [136]. <!-- claim: 442946e67be650c9; evidence: 77a8d41b0d0db525; source: 5734e0647b671686 --> The span-event semantic conventions for exceptions are deprecated: use semantic conventions for exceptions in logs instead [135]. <!-- claim: 3b56a8f949e64002; evidence: 0655c35981238247; source: 524de37ac82038e5 -->

*Synthesis.* Single-statement handler patterns suit linters and analyzers; review attention belongs on the cross-layer classes no lane owns today (lost causes, boundary translation, handling twice, cleanup masking, library code configuring logging), guided by a per-language canon table with a "do not flag" column (deliberate Go opacity, PEP 8's log-and-reraise, documented `expect`); log-and-rethrow should land low. Version facts (Go 1.13/1.20/1.26, Python 3.11, Rust 1.65, .NET 10, ES2022/2027, OTel semconv 1.44) are listed with evidence in [D5](dossiers/D5-errors-rust-go-python.md) and [D6](dossiers/D6-errors-js-jvm-dotnet-cpp-swift-otel.md).

### F7 — Contract-first / interface-first decomposition for parallel work

The speed-up was stated as an expected benefit, not measured. ### F8 — Review-time enforcement and LLM-generated code

### F11 — Do design-guidance instructions change LLM code structure?
<!-- facet: F11 -->

They do, with trade-offs. Increasing the specificity of OOD guidance tends to produce projects with more classes and higher total complexity, size, and coupling [237]. <!-- claim: 32adafcfc45663f5; evidence: 5020c71de7b276f1; ## Synthesis

1. *The practices are levers on change cost, so make the expected change explicit.* Across F1–F5 the evidence converges on one operational idea: a design is good relative to the changes it must absorb. Superpowers already records boundaries (`owns:`/`consumes:`) and contracts (seam beads); extending these with the hidden decision, promised behaviors, error contract and stated variation per unit turns design taste into checkable statements.
2. *Enforcement must be anchored, or it will be noise.* Review literature (F8) and the repository's recorded roasts agree that findings without a stated contract or observable consequence are discounted. Giving the PR roast the design contracts is the first hypothesis to test — cheapest probe first (the spec as stated intent) — and any new lane should prove its precision in shadow mode before it can drive fixes, because a binding contract can also create rigidity and Blocking inflation.
3. *Error handling is the most evidence-backed new coverage.* Outage-class handler defects (F6) are concrete, mostly observable in a diff, and currently unowned in super-roast; the language canon is clear enough to encode with "do not flag" exceptions.
4. *Contract-first is an extension, not a stage, and its speed-up is unproven.* The literature (F7) says interfaces fixed early do not remove coordination and pay only when stable; the repository's recorded parallelism loss came from edge rules on Markdown heading names, not from a missing stage; and stacked consumers of a changing contract can drift silently today. Measure and close that gap first.
5. *Prompts move structure but not reliably.* F11 evidence rules out relying on design exhortations; concrete fields and verification are the robust levers, consistent with the repository's prior eval of the `Interfaces:` block.
6. *Connection to the in-round step-back request.* Its reported late defects (legacy consumers left behind; missing failure-path guarantees; evidence lifecycle) were surfaced by the existing holistic step-back once it ran, and its remedy is a cadence change. When it is implemented, stable seam notes and recurring design `[class]` clusters are candidate inputs and triggers (recommendations §12). It is not implemented here.

## Limitations

- **Retrieval stop: budget-exhausted (round 5).** Facet readiness held after round 3; two lead residual rounds still found new relevant sources (agentic review acceptance [271], non-functional quality of LLM code [272], industrial false-alarm reduction [273]), so the coverage-saturated rule was not met. Remaining gaps (from `coverage.json.stop.remaining_gaps`): the TSE 2022 stack-trace logging study and other logging-practice studies; Effective Java and Framework Design Guidelines wrapping guidance; any measurement of interface-first speed-up; architectural documentation as LLM input; controlled decomposition/comprehension experiments behind bot walls; Wendorff 2001 and Vokáč 2004 originals; empirical Go/Rust interface-practice studies; live precision of LLM design findings; severity-label tests.
- **Evidence quality.** Most design-metric evidence is correlational and size-confounded; key experiments are old, small, student-based, or Java/C++-only; much 2025–2026 LLM evidence is preprint or vendor material and model-specific. Practitioner sources are labelled in the dossiers.
- **Repository evidence is thin where it matters most.** Both recorded runs edited this repository's Markdown skills, not OOP application code; contract beads have never been exercised on code; no wall-clock or per-lane precision data exist.
- **Verification limits.** Quotes were verified mechanically against fetched text (exact, 5-gram fuzzy, or despaced matching for PDF artifacts) and 13 by lead inspection; 9 candidates were rejected. Seven evidence rows came through WebFetch and are not anchored as verbatim claims. The claim-support check is lexical, not semantic; the lead reviewed material claims for entailment.
- **Recommendations are untested.** No skill, engine, test or prompt was changed; all expected effects await the evals in recommendations §9. The plan was revised once after an independent adversarial critique ([critique/critique-1.md](critique/critique-1.md), [critique/refinement-log.md](critique/refinement-log.md)); v2 was not sent back for a second critique round.
